Open Agent Hackathon 2026 · Architecture v0.8 · Track 02 – The Agent That Can Explain Why

Haze Agent: Evidence to Action

A programme that investigates haze harm, explains the evidence and its causes, compares feasible interventions and tests a clearly defined 10% reduction target. The hackathon builds one executable slice: a Klang maintenance crew’s 08:00–12:00 shift, planned by the four-role harness and compared against its own baseline using a named exposure proxy. Above it, four continuing departments coordinate research, planning, validation and experiments. Beyond it, two expansion paths—worker protection and source prevention—reuse the same foundation, but each one has to pass its own evidence gates.

§3The 10% contract

“Reduce harm by 10%” means nothing until it says 10% of what, for whom, over which period, against which baseline. Three outcomes are easy to confuse, and each supports a different claim.

OutcomeMeaningSupported claim
Ambient pollutionOutdoor concentration or a defined fire-attributable environmental measureA change in the named measure, with source attribution where claimed
Human exposureConcentration encountered by a defined population across locations and activitiesA modeled or measured exposure change; a health benefit needs its own model
DamageSpecified health, economic, environmental or climate consequencesA scoped estimate or evaluated change under an applicable accounting method

Hackathon default. One Klang maintenance crew, 08:00–12:00 local, measured with the concentration-time exposure proxy (§6). Compare feasible plans for the same required work under the same environmental scenario, and test whether at least a 10% projected improvement is supported. If it is not, that is a valid result.

Record before enabling any target claim. target_id/version, owner and objective, metric and units, geography and population, fire/event and assessment periods, baseline, accounting coverage, available actions, budget, constraints, method and model versions, uncertainty, and the review and measurement plan. Every expansion gets its own contract: a field pilot keeps its crew metric, a prevention programme defines treated sources and downwind populations, and a damage programme selects categories and valuation rules.

§6Interventions and scenarios

Scientific estimation is an explicit tool service, separate from language generation and from evidence storage. The pilot needs two formulas, both computed by versioned deterministic tools and never by a model’s prose.

Crew exposure proxy

E = Σ segments ( workers × duration × effective concentration )
units: person-hours × µg/m³

This is a concentration-time proxy. It is not inhaled dose, a safe-duration rule or a health-damage estimate. Missing coverage stays a gap; it is never treated as a zero concentration.

Projected reduction

R = (B − I) / B
B = baseline estimate, I = intervention estimate
same metric, population and assessment horizon
report 100 × R only when B is positive, valid, comparable

Overlapping controls are never added as percentages, and a study result is never carried beyond the setting it supports.

StepRequired behaviour
Fix a baselineVersion the no-intervention schedule; keep the same environmental scenario and common task requirements for every alternative
Construct feasible actionsExplicit timing, staffing, availability, cost, capacity and guidance constraints; surface rescheduling, lost work and transferred exposure
Estimate effectsReviewed effectiveness assumptions run through the exposure tools; no invented safe durations, equipment or approved closures
Compare uncertaintyModel-supported intervals or sensitivity scenarios; forecast interval, parameter uncertainty and analyst-chosen range kept distinct
Rank and explainFeasible alternatives, expected effect, resources, distribution of benefit and the assumption that changes the ranking

A scenario output carries scenario_id/version, target_id, baseline_ref, input snapshot, model and parameter versions, actions, feasibility findings, result units, reduction, uncertainty basis, assumptions and review status. Workplace comparisons share common ambient inputs. Prevention comparisons hold exogenous weather comparable while allowing emissions and pollution to change.

Model and parameter registry

Every scientific tool records model_id/version, intended use, input and output schemas, references, parameter sources, applicability, review status, evaluation evidence and uncertainty method. The hackathon ships the scoped exposure proxy only. Haze attribution needs a defined non-haze counterfactual; health impact needs a reviewed relationship with baseline rates and expert support; economic impact needs a stated consequence, valuation year, horizon and accounting rules.

§7Validation and experiments

EvidenceCritic keeps its authority: it independently retrieves pinned evidence through Zetaris and returns approve, revise or insufficient_evidence. v0.8 widens what it checks, from source support to method, calculation and interpretation. A second model’s opinion is not scientific validation on its own; checks are deterministic, reproduced, benchmarked or reviewed by an expert, and the record says which.

Review layerWhat it checks
Source and claim supportExact revisions, passage-to-claim support, source quality, conflicts, corrections, scope and freshness
Method and model applicabilityPopulation, geography, exposure period, study design, model assumptions, whether the method supports the inference
Calculation correctnessReproducible inputs, units, aggregation, baselines, missingness, uncertainty, sensitivity, valid percentages
Experiment interpretationTestable hypothesis, comparison method, implementation fidelity, external changes, limits of causal conclusions
Publication and execution gateExact artifact binding, required reviews, current evidence, configured permissions, separate operational authorization
Supported is not authorized. Being scientifically supported does not mean being operationally authorized, and permission to run a pilot does not show that its hypothesis is correct. Every review records the reviewer or review execution, the artifact, evidence and model versions, the results, any unresolved findings and its expiry. Any substantive revision invalidates an approval it is incompatible with.

Experiment lifecycle

hypothesis → protocol → independent design review → simulation or authorized field pilot → implementation record → observations → comparative evaluation → independent result review → reviewed knowledge update

ArtifactRequired content
Experiment protocolHypothesis, metric, baseline or comparison, population, period, measurements, resources, owner, review requirements, stopping criteria
Implementation recordWhat was authorized, what was actually done, when and by whom, controls used, deviations, task movement, delivery evidence
Observation setMeasured values, calibration and coverage, units, timing, provenance, missingness, relevant environmental or operational changes
Outcome reportPredicted effects, observed changes, comparative estimate, uncertainty, limitations, fidelity, whether attribution is justified

A simulation tests behaviour under a model; a historical replay tests the pipeline using only information available then. Neither establishes the real-world benefit of an unimplemented intervention. Validated findings may update rankings, model assumptions or H-MEM lessons, with prior versions and the review disposition kept; rejected or uncertain findings remain retrievable as such.

§8Continuing operation

The programme runs for days; individual runs stay bounded, recoverable and auditable. “Continuously operating” means scheduled checks and meaningful event triggers, never an unbounded thought loop. A run ends on completion, exhausted budget or deadline, an unresolved dependency, an explicit pause or a decision only a person can make, and work that is waiting does not hold a specialist slot.

Programme responsibilityRequired behaviour
Goals and targetsVersioned target contract, supported scope, baseline and current delivery stage; unanswered questions kept apart from accepted findings
Research backlogQuestions, hypotheses, missing evidence, candidate interventions and experiment proposals, each with owner, priority and dependencies
Budget and schedulingProgramme and per-job resources; reserved review and synthesis capacity; cadence, deadlines, stop conditions, pause and resume
Cross-department handoffBounded tasks to existing profiles and tools, exact artifact references, independent review requirements, escalation
User accountabilityDecisions needed, source gaps, failures, revisions and progress; changes to scope, metric, budget or permission become explicit programme revisions
Freshness is a visible contract. For every source the workspace shows the last attempted check, the last successful check, the newest source timestamp, the accepted-through watermark, the next check and any known gaps. No monitor guarantees it will see every new paper or news item. Re-fetching an old record does not reset its observation age, and a failed source stays visibly stale or partial.

§9Shared evidence

There is still one canonical factual foundation. The domain is wider now: World Model holds programmes, districts, stations, observations, forecast runs, papers, guidance, scientific model versions, scenarios, experiments, implementations and conclusion revisions, all with stable IDs and rebuildable projections.

ComponentResponsibility
World Model write pathNormalize, resolve stable IDs, validate provenance, append evidence revisions, rebuild projections; owns canonical evidence and decision state
World Model read pathScoped immutable snapshots and entity context; versioned views registered with Zetaris; projection watermarks checked before publication
H-MEM captureReviewed decisions, corrections, preferences and procedural lessons with scope, review state, provenance and evidence links
H-MEM recall and auditMemory IDs, scope, retrieval rationale and supporting revisions; capture, read, feedback and supersession audited

Time semantics

  • Observation interval, publication or issue time, applicability interval, retrieval time and system acceptance time are stored separately.
  • UTC plus original timezone metadata on every record.
  • Concentrations, indices and aggregation periods are never mixed.

Lifecycle

  • Conclusion states: current, needs_review, stale, superseded, withdrawn, insufficient_evidence.
  • Research acceptance is a separate state; an updated paper never silently overwrites a model or an old estimate.
  • A correction or retraction marks affected conclusions and linked memories for review.

Reviewed memory is not live science. Before factual memory is reused, its scope, validity, supporting evidence and conclusion status are checked. H-MEM ranking confidence is not scientific confidence, and automatic promotion of synthesized lessons stays off for the first pilot.

§15Demo and acceptance

Demo statement. “When evidence changes, Haze Agent revises a feasible work decision and shows the consequence for crew exposure.” The demo shows a supported result even when the 10% target is infeasible. It never forces a disagreement or manufactures a winning percentage.

1 · PlanSupported crew request, fixed task requirements, pinned snapshot; baseline and two or three feasible alternatives with modeled exposure and operational trade-offs
2 · ExplainOpen a recommendation to its source records, guidance, units, calculation inputs, assumptions and uncertainty

4 · UpdateA later relevant observation or forecast changes the affected plan with an explicit old/new reason; an unrelated update changes nothing

Evaluation layers stay separate. Calculation and coverage correctness, feasible completion, support, update correctness, latency and cost are measured here. Forecast validation, scientific model validation and intervention effectiveness need their own tests. Testing includes at least one negative case where no improvement is supported.

Source prevention and its action contract

Start with one landscape, one feasible intervention and an operator who can carry it out. The first candidate mechanism to investigate is peatland water-management inspection, maintenance or repair with a qualified local partner, plus fire-free land preparation where locally supported. The action is chosen only after checking hydrology, land use, operating authority, livelihoods, resources and evidence of effectiveness. Restoration findings vary with climate, ecosystem and intervention type [E2].

§18Evaluation, activation and readiness

Three questions need three different comparisons. Keeping them apart is what stops a fire-attribution estimate from being reported as programme benefit, or a good programme from being credited to the agent.

QuestionRequired comparison
Fire attributionSelected consequences under justified with-fire and no-fire counterfactuals; pollution attribution covers the smoke subset only
Programme effectDelivered programme against ordinary management under comparable conditions, including weather and implementation differences
Agent added valueAgent-assisted planning against simple rules or competent expert planning with the same options, evidence access and budget

Evaluation happens in three stages. A replay tests behaviour using only information available at the time, and a shadow run checks the data, usability and the feasibility of recommendations. A controlled pilot then assesses delivered actions and comparative outcomes. Replay and shadow alone never establish avoided real-world harm. For peatland work, the evaluation has to account for connected hydrology, because adjacent plots may share effects.
